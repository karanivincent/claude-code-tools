# Pictures that blend: prompt rules

How to ask an image model (Higgsfield `gpt_image_2_6`, 2k, high, 9:16) for pictures of one place that a moving camera
will project, warp or blend. Learned from two rounds of hall pictures on the ancient jar test (7 November 2026).

## A prompt that worked (the master wide)

Four labelled blocks, the same words in every picture of the place:

- WORLD: the place as a real (or painted) location, every fixed thing named where it stands; "not a cartoon, not an
  illustration" for realistic, or "painted storybook film still" for the painted style.
- CAMERA: where it stands on the plan, what it looks at, its height against something in the frame, the lens as a
  field of view in words, horizon at the middle, vertical lines perfectly straight.
- LIGHT: named sources ("warm candlelight and firelight, cool daylight through the windows from the back"), even
  exposure edge to edge.
- RULES: everything sharp front to back, no fog, no haze, no vignette, no lens flare, no people, no animals, no text;
  the edges show plain wall, column and floor, nothing important cut by an edge.

For every other picture of the place, give the master as an image reference (upload with `media_upload`, then
`medias: [{ role: "image_references" }]`) and say "exactly the same place as the reference, the camera moved to ...".
Cost: 2.75 credits a picture.


What a skill should do when it asks an image model for several pictures of one place that a moving camera will blend.
Learned from both rounds.

### What to state

1. A floor plan with numbers: the room's width, depth and height, and where the fixed things stand (the throne at the
   back wall, steps 3 m deep, the arcades 3.5 m either side of the middle, the runner 2.2 m wide down the middle). Every
   picture gets the same paragraph, word for word. Pictures agree only on what the prompt pins down.
2. Each picture's camera as a position on that plan plus a direction ("standing at the middle of the entrance, 20 m
   from the throne, looking straight at it"), not as an angle on its own. "Turned 30 degrees" came out as 5 degrees
   twice; a target ("looking at the left fire bowl") is followed better.
3. The lens as a field of view in words ("the throne and both candelabra fill the middle third of the width"), with the
   millimetres as a second hint. Both rounds came out narrower than asked.
4. The eye height, with something in the frame to measure it by ("the camera at the height of the fire bowls' rims").
   Bare "1.6 m" came out at 2.3-3.8 m.
5. Horizontal, level, straight verticals, everything sharp, no haze, no vignette, no depth of field. All of these held;
   keep them.
6. The same light in every picture, named by its sources ("daylight from the clerestory windows high on the left, warm
   torches on the columns"). This held too.

### What to forbid

- Fine repeated detail: criss-cross braces, many rails, slats, fences, rows of poles. Ask for "a few big, simple
  shapes" instead; the trestle picture with dozens of braces made every bridge shot crowded (train heist).

- Anything that moves between pictures: people, open doors, objects that are not on the plan.
- Props near the frame's edges or close to the camera (the lily vase, the potted plant, the near column with its
  torch). They are drawn where only one picture sees them, so they smear when projected and cannot match in a blend.
- Redrawing the room for a close-up: no extra steps, no moved fire bowls, no different canopy. Say "the same throne as
  the reference, closer" and give the reference.
- A wider or narrower room than the plan, a symmetric room when it is not, and decorative repetition that differs
  between pictures (banners, ivy).

### How to chain references

- Make the wide first and treat it as the master. Every other picture gets the master as an image reference, and so
  does the plan paragraph.
- Ask for each new view as "the same room as the reference, the camera moved to …"; never describe the room again in
  other words.
- A close-up should be a crop-and-push of the master's content. Ideally it is generated by enlarging the master's crop
  and re-detailing it (an upscale or img2img at low strength), not drawn fresh. That is the only way the throne stays
  where it was.
- Check each picture before the next. Fit its camera (horizon, runner width, the fixed landmarks); if the fit error is
  over about 15 px at 2688 px tall, or the height or lens is off by more than 20%, redo it before drawing more.

### Which views a camera journey needs

- One wide down the main axis from behind the furthest camera position. This carries most moves in G, including a
  50-degree orbit.
- One close-up for each place the camera gets within about a third of the room's depth of (here the throne). Without
  it, the wide is upscaled 5 times.
- Side views only if the journey turns more than about 40 degrees or slides sideways more than a quarter of the room's
  width. They must be turned for real (30 degrees or more); a 5-degree side view adds ghosts, not coverage.
- A reverse view only if the camera ever looks back toward the entrance.
- G needs the wide and the close-ups only. F needs every view the journey passes through, and a fitted camera for each.

### Edge and overlap rules

- Draw 15-20% more than the camera will see on every side: more vault above, more floor below, more wall at the sides.
  A projection or a depth mesh runs out exactly where the camera swings, at the frame's edge.
- Neighbouring views should share at least half their picture, with the shared part plain (wall, floor, columns) so it
  matches.
- Keep busy, hard-to-match detail (banners, tapestries, clutter) away from the overlaps.
- Only change pictures inside fast motion (a whip, a swing, a fast push) or a cut or dissolve, and spread the blend
  over the whole move. In slow motion use one picture, even if it is the less good one.
- Colour-match before blending, measured on content every picture shows (the same object), never on the whole frame.
