# Lessons across the channel: brief for the synthesis agent

We studied a channel's best recent Shorts, one report each, to learn how they are made so that we can make original
videos of our own. Your job: read every report in full and write the lessons that hold across
them. Not a summary of each video: what is true of most of them, what varies and why, and what that means for us.

Inputs (read all of them, in full):
- Every survey report: {study}/<id>/analysis/survey.md for every id in {study}/{survey}/picked.txt (id|views|-|title).
- Any full four-part studies of the same channel ({study}/<id>/analysis/part1.md … part4.md) and their measures.
- Every measures.json beside those reports (pace, cuts, stillness, sharpness, fine edges, saturation, brightness, LUFS).
- Our kit: {skills}/toon-world/SKILL.md (looks scene by scene, game world, parts plan, people),
  {skills}/toon-world/templates/gameworld/README.md, {skills}/toon-video/SKILL.md (story, meaning sheet, backbone and
  dynamics, checks), {skills}/toon-video/references/playbook.md.
You may open a few frames to settle a disagreement between reports; say when you did.

Write {study}/{survey}/lessons.md, plain English, short sentences, every
claim tied to the videos it comes from (short names, e.g. "pink prison, burglar, grave bell"). Sections:

1. **The backbone**: what nearly every video does (count them: "18 of 20"). Story, opening, ending, pace, joins,
   colour, people, text on things, sound. This becomes the starting point for every video we make.
2. **Story shapes**: each shape he uses (con revealed, true mechanism, "levels of", X versus Y, what-if, viewer as
   hero, experiment with a catch, …), which videos, views, and the beats each shape needs. Which shapes fit Bible
   stories and which fit Epiclings (clever heists, cons, escapes and last stands from history).
3. **The dials that vary**: a table across all the videos of the measures (pace, hard cuts, still share, sharpness,
   fine edges, saturation, brightness, words a minute, LUFS) with views. Then what makes them vary (night versus day,
   faces versus machines, interiors versus landscapes) and the target ranges we should use per kind of story.
4. **Which look for which scene**: a decision guide in our terms (game world, picture room, flat 2D place, drawn in
   code; picture sky; ink lines on the world or not), built from what he does in each kind of place: interiors,
   towns and streets, landscapes and sea, machines, diagrams and explanations, crowds, night. Say plainly where his
   look differs from our current defaults (ink outlines on the 3D world; cleaner shapes rather than fine texture;
   saturation over sharpness) and recommend what we should do.
5. **Joins and camera**: the catalogue of joins made by the world (through glass, gaps, screens, icons, smoke,
   impact holes, shape matches), how often each appears, and the rule we should take.
6. **Teaching the mechanism**: how he shows the "how it works" moment without charts (on the real object, a place
   for the idea, a single wide shot, labels flipping on the word), with examples.
7. **People**: the drawings and states that recur (back view, seated, lying, carrying, lip sync, costume changes,
   crowds, animals) ranked by how many videos need them.
8. **What we are missing**: one ranked table. Each gap: what it is, how many of the videos need it, its kind
   (story, plot, detail, colour, people, effects, camera, sound, assets), size (small under a day, medium a few days,
   large a new system), and whether we have it built, as a blueprint, or not at all. The top of this table is our
   build order.
9. **Can we make videos like these?** A table: video, views, the verdict from its report, the one thing that blocks it.
   Then the honest overall answer.
10. **Rules of ours his videos challenge**: each as a choice for Vincent with a recommendation (for example our
    narration pace of about 150 words a minute against his 210–245; ink lines on the world; sharpness targets per
    story; "no 3D model of anything alive" against his galloping horses). Vincent decides; do not change any rule.
11. **New playbook rows**: the techniques that are not yet in the playbook, deduplicated across reports, each with
    why it works, a short blueprint, and the videos it is seen in, formatted as rows for the playbook's tables
    (Story; Camera and joins; Look and light; People; Things that act).

Then a short **For the next videos** section: the ten most useful things for the stories we are about to make, in
our current kit, with nothing new to build beyond small jobs.

Work in the foreground, edit nothing outside {survey}/, never post anything. Final message: the path and a
10-line summary.
