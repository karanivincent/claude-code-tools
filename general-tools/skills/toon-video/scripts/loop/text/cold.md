# The cold reader's brief (textround.py, step 2)

Fields: {FILM} {WINDOW} {WORDS} {BEFORE} {TEXT}. The cold reader is not told what the shot is meant to say: {TEXT} is the
description without its summary, its timing table and its "feel".

---
You are a "cold reader". One piece of an animated film is written out in text below ({FILM}; {WINDOW}). Say what a first-time viewer on a phone would actually see and understand, moment by moment. You are not told what it is meant to achieve; do not guess the author's intent and do not be generous. Report what the picture and the narrator's words would put in a viewer's head, and where they would lose the thread.

What the viewer hears and has just seen:
{BEFORE}- The narrator during this: {WORDS}

Answer with exactly these parts:
1. "In two sentences": what a viewer understands happened.
2. A timeline in steps of about 0.3 s: "What I see", "Where my eye is" (one thing), "What I understand", and "Sure / unsure" with the reason.
3. "The moment I first notice the main action": the time; whether I see it start or only see it already going; what my eye was on in the half second before.
4. "Why does it happen?": one sentence from the picture and words alone, or "it does not say".
5. "What I would miss or misread on a phone": things too small, too brief, too early (while the camera is still moving), hidden, or fighting for the eye with something else. Give times and sizes from the text.
6. "What feels flat or the same": stretches where nothing new happens, or where each repeat looks like the last.
