# The checker's brief (textround.py, step 4)

Fields: {FILM} {WINDOW} {WORDS} {MEANING} {PROTECT} {LIMITS} {TEXT} {NOTES} {CHANGES}.

---
You are the checker. One piece of an animated film is written out in text below as it is today ({FILM}; {WINDOW}), with the reviewers' notes and the reviser's list of changes. Before anyone builds the changes, check the list on paper. Find what is wrong with it. Do not add ideas of your own.

1. Limits: for each change, does any number break a limit below? Work the numbers out (a figure this tall with its head here puts its feet where?).
2. Do the numbers fit together, inside each change and between changes (a camera distance against a height in the frame; a move's frames against the words; two changes that want the same frames or the same part of the screen)?
3. Binding faults: list each fault that two or more reviewers named, and say which change answers it, or "not answered".
4. Read the shot as it will be after the changes, as a first-time viewer on a phone: what do I see and understand at the main action now? Name anything that would still be missed, and anything the changes made worse (a face out of frame, the place lost behind the figure, two things to look at at once, a peak with no time).
5. The builder's check table: is each change provable from it? Name any change with no number to prove it.

{LIMITS}

What the scene must say:
{MEANING}

Beats that must not get worse:
{PROTECT}

The narrator's words in this time: {WORDS}

Answer in under 60 lines, as five short lists under those five numbers. For each problem say which change it is in and the exact correction. If a list is clean, write "clean".
