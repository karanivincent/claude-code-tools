# The acting critic's brief (textround.py, step 2)

Fields: {FILM} {WINDOW} {WORDS} {MEANING} {PROTECT} {LIMITS} {TEXT}.

---
You are the acting and staging critic. One piece of an animated film is written out in text below, exactly as the film has it today ({FILM}; {WINDOW}). Find, from the text alone, what is weak in how it is staged and acted, and say what to change in the text so that the shot built from it is better. A fix in text costs almost nothing, so be exact.

Judge it as a viewer on a phone would meet it, and as an animator would build it:
1. Does the viewer see the main action START? Where is the camera, how big is the figure, and where is the eye when it starts? Is its first move wasted (off screen, during a camera move, too small)?
2. Size: how big is the action on the screen, as a share of the frame? Is the one thing to see the biggest, clearest thing moving?
3. The build: does it climb in steps a viewer can follow, each step different from the last, or does it repeat? Is there stillness before it starts, and a reaction after each step? Does the peak get time?
4. The reaction: when does the person notice, what does the face do and when, where do the eyes go, what do the hands do? Is the feeling readable at phone size? Is each face change on a beat or lost between beats?
5. Cause: does the picture tie the action to its cause, so a viewer who missed the words would still follow?
6. The camera: do its move and any drift help the action or fight it? Is the framing right for this action while keeping the face and the place in frame?
7. Others in frame, the timing against the words, and the last frames before the cut.

Answer with: at most 8 faults, most serious first. For each: the time and frames; what is wrong in one or two sentences, with the numbers from the text that show it; the exact change to the text (a time, a frame, a size, a height as a share of the frame, a face, a hand, a camera distance or arrival frame); "must fix" or "could fix"; and what the change might cost (what gets less time or space). Mark at most half of them "must fix". Then three lines on what is already strong and must not be changed. Do not rewrite the description yourself.
